The GENOME **source code** is free software under the Apache License 2.0. The GENOME
**name** is not part of that grant — Apache-2.0 §6 explicitly withholds trademark rights.

This policy exists so you know exactly what you may do without asking. Most of what
people want to do is permitted, and this document leads with that rather than with a list
of prohibitions.

## What you may do without asking

- **Say your software uses GENOME.** "Built with GENOME", "powered by GENOME",
  "compatible with GENOME", "a GENOME plugin" — all fine, including in commercial
  products. This is nominative use and we encourage it.
- **Use the code commercially.** Apache-2.0 grants that unconditionally. Sell products
  built on GENOME, embed it in a closed-source application, run it for paying customers.
  The license means what it says.
- **Fork the repository** and keep the name in your fork's history, README attribution,
  and package metadata that describes what it was derived from.
- **Write about GENOME** — articles, talks, tutorials, benchmarks, critical reviews. You
  do not need our permission, and you do not need to show us a draft. If you benchmark
  GENOME, please state the version and method so readers can reproduce it.
- **Use the name in academic work.** See [`CITATION.cff`](./CITATION.cff).

## What requires written permission

- **Naming a distributed product, service, or package "GENOME"**, or a name confusingly
  similar to it. If you publish a modified version, rename it. You may say "derived from
  GENOME"; you may not call it GENOME.
- **Offering a hosted or managed service under the GENOME name.** Run a fork as a service
  freely — just not under our name.
- **Using the marks or logos in a way that implies endorsement, affiliation, sponsorship,
  or partnership** with the Owner where none exists.
- **Registering domains, trademarks, package names, or social handles** containing GENOME,
  Northtek, or FrostByte in a way likely to cause confusion.

## Why the line is where it is

A trademark protects users, not the owner's ego: it lets someone who installs "GENOME"
know they got the thing we build and support. Restricting the *name* is what makes the
*code* safe to give away without conditions — which is why the license is Apache-2.0 and
intends to stay that way.
